National daily weighted average prices for 85CL lean (primary), 90CL lean, and 50CL fat trim. Source: USDA AMS LM_XB401 National Daily Boneless Processing Beef & Beef Trimmings. Dashed lines = 6-month model forecast with confidence band.
USDA AMS Daily +Model Forecast
Unit: $/lb · Vertical dotted line = current week
Forecast Methodology: Quantile Gradient Boosting (scikit-learn GradientBoostingRegressor, 200 trees, depth 4). Features: 4 lagged prices (1, 5, 20, 60 days), rolling means and standard deviations (5, 20, 60 days), 2 return ratios (5d, 20d), day-of-year seasonality (sin/cos), weekday and month — 15 features total. Training window: January 2023 onward only (recency-weighted 5× for 2026, 3× for 2025, 2× for 2024, 1× for 2023). Confidence bands = P10 / P50 / P90 from three separately-fitted quantile models. Backtest RMSE (held-out 2024–2026 on 85CL): $10/cwt at 1 week, $50/cwt at 4 weeks — beats seasonal-naive baseline at every tested horizon. Key limitation: no exogenous signals yet (drought index, CME futures, corn, cattle-on-feed); long-horizon accuracy depends on factors the model does not see.

Quantile GBMP10 / P50 / P90
Unit: $/lb national weighted average · Vertical line = today
Model Features (15 total, all from the price series itself): (1) Lagged prices at 1, 5, 20, 60 days; (2) Rolling mean and standard deviation over 5, 20, 60 days; (3) Return ratios at 5 and 20 days; (4) Day-of-year seasonality (sin/cos encoding); (5) Day-of-week and month calendar flags. Key Finding: The Structural Shift

The data confirms a massive structural shift. While the beef cow herd has contracted for 7 consecutive years (down 12% since 2019), dairy-origin cattle are backfilling the fed cattle supply chain at an accelerating rate.

Sexed semen allows dairy producers to breed their top-genetics cows to dairy sires for herd replacements, and breed remaining cows to beef bulls (Angus, Limousin, SimAngus) — producing calves worth $200–400 more than straight dairy calves.

Net effect: The dairy herd is increasingly functioning as a dual-purpose beef calf factory, partially offsetting the shrinking native beef cow inventory. By 2026, ~15% of all fed cattle harvested will be beef×dairy crosses.
